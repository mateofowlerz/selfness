# Startups vs Labs

The best AI products released in the past four years have been built by the labs themselves. This is something I wouldn’t have predicted if you’d asked me in 2023, and I find it quite sad that **small startups weren’t the ones releasing products like Claude Code**.
This happens because the *model provider* can **use RL to train models** specifically for the products it wants to release. The labs did it with coding, and [OpenAI just did it with computer use](https://openai.com/index/gpt-6-astra/).

(If your counterargument is Cursor, [they sold to SpaceX because they realized this](https://cursor.com/blog/spacex-model-training?utm_source=chatgpt.com).)

It’s weird that the *best product of this decade* was made by big companies and **not** by a small, *lean startup* that uses the big lab as a *platform*. The triumph of Claude Code/Codex is incomparable with Granola (though is a great product).

My prediction for the next four years is that **this will change**. In coding, **we’re close to the “good enough” stage for most tasks**, which means a startup could use open-weight models **in the same way labs use their proprietary models**. A small company with deep pockets could build the *next great product*. This will become increasingly **true as models get better**: you really **don’t need Fable** to automate *email tasks*. A startup using Kimi fine-tuned with RL can build a better solution than OpenAI today, because for many tasks, **capabilities are no longer the bottleneck**.

Five years ago, if you’d wanted to build the *next great product*, you would have needed to be at Anthropic or OpenAI. I believe that has changed, **once and for all**.

Part of me also wants to believe this, because a world where centralization is necessary for innovation is one I wouldn’t want to be part of. The best possible time to do a startup?
